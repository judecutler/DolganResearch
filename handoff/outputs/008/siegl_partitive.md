## What it says for Study C

1. **Siegl counts the Dolgan partitive, on a small scale.** §2.5 "Der dolganische Partitiv und seine Frequenz" (pp. 372–375) counts tokens in a written corpus of about 48,000 words: 14 tokens, 12 of them imperative. There is 1 token (also imperative) in about 30 minutes of transcribed narrative. He gives no table, no percentages and no clause-type breakdown beyond "imperative", and says the data are not statistically reliable:
   > "auch wenn ein manuell erstelltes Korpus keine statistisch belastbaren Daten präsentieren kann" (p. 372)
   > "gerade einmal mit 14 Belegen zu Buche schlägt, davon 12 im Imperativ" (p. 394)
2. **Everything else is qualitative.** The partitive "zumeist im Kontext des Imperativs oder des Futurs" (PDF p. 6), framed as irrealis. Partitive objects with conditional forms come from the earlier grammars. Apart from one exception (his ex. 17b), they are not in his text corpus or field notes, but he found some in samples of the Hamburg corpus (pp. 358–359). Future with the partitive is accepted in elicitation but is rare in texts (p. 375).
3. **He says a dedicated study is still needed:**
   > "eine belastbare Aussage ohne eine speziale Studie ... vorerst nicht möglich" (p. 375)
4. **Evenki (§3.3.2, pp. 378–379): this conflicts with the Study C premise.** He says there is no specialised study of the use and text frequency of the Evenki indefinite accusative, but he cites Kazama (2012: 144–145) as finding that it does prefer future and imperative:
   > "die Verwendung des indefiniten Akkusativs eindeutig eine Präferenz für Futur und Imperativ zeigt (Kazama 2012: 144–145)" (p. 379)
   Kazama, Shinjiro. 2012. Designative case in Tungusic languages. In Andrej L. Malchukov & Lindsay J. Whaley (eds.), *Recent advances in Tungusic linguistics* (Turcologica 89), 123–152. Wiesbaden: Harrassowitz.
5. Footnote 5 (p. 351): the Hamburg (INEL) Dolgan corpus was not accessible when he wrote the study. A few samples from it showed no deviations from his results, but a final evaluation is outstanding. His counts are therefore not INEL counts.
6. No mention of generation or speaker age with the partitive ("Generation" 0, "jünger" 0; "Alter" twice, on PDF pp. 5–6, not about speakers).
