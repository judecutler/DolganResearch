## Q1. Who has published on Dolgan, and what?

### Takeaway
Dolgan has one modern reference grammar, Däbritz (2022, Brill), which is corpus-based. It also has a contact-focused dissertation (Stapert 2013) and a scatter of short topic studies. Most of the topic studies are by Däbritz and Florian Siegl. They cover possession, imperatives, placeholders, negation, the partitive, quantifiers, deontic *naada*, information structure and the habitual. Older Russian and Polish work covers description, the lexicon, word formation and loanwords: Ubryatova, Artemyev, Aksenova, and Marek and Kamil Stachowski. Since 2022 the new work is mostly lexical (Evenki loans), phonetic (vowel harmony) or sociolinguistic (Anabar).

### Inferences
- The two most active authors are Däbritz and Siegl. Their work is description and typology of single constructions. Almost none of it is quantitative, variationist, or uses the CS/BOR-Morph tiers systematically.
- The Hamburg team built the INEL tiers for code-switching, borrowing morphology and predication. Apart from Däbritz's own information-structure and predication work, I found no published study that exploits the BOR-Morph or CS tiers.

The explicit open questions I did find come from Däbritz (2020 FULL, 2023 JEL), Siegl (2015, 2020) and Stachowski (2011). ## Q3. Topics well studied for Sakha but not for Dolgan

### Takeaway
Sakha has formal-syntactic work on case and agreement, argument structure and lexical categories (Baker & Vinokurova; Vinokurova 2005), and contact work with Even (Pakendorf). For Dolgan, the equivalent topics have only descriptive grammar coverage (Däbritz 2022) or none that I could find.

### Cited Findings
- Sakha case assignment as two modalities, dependent case plus agreement: Baker & Vinokurova (2010), *NLLT*. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11049-010-9105-1)
- Sakha partitive as structural case: Baker & Vinokurova, "Rethinking structural case: Partitive case in Sakha" (Zenodo; Sheifer's slides cite it as 2021). — [OpenPolar/Zenodo](https://openpolar.no/Record/ftdatacite:10.5281%2Fzenodo.4680300)
- Sakha lexical categories and argument structure: Vinokurova (2005), LOT dissertation, Utrecht. — [Utrecht portal](https://research-portal.uu.nl/en/publications/lexical-categories-and-argument-structure-a-study-with-reference-/)

- Sakha *naada* compared with Dolgan, finding the cognate languages diverge: Siegl (2021). — [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/38cc0c4fe5454b46bd6a4697e9a7d12a)

### Inferences
- A Baker & Vinokurova-style test of dependent case on Dolgan corpus data is a natural transfer. It would ask whether Dolgan's ternary DOM and partitive pattern like Sakha's. The INEL SyF/SeR tiers and the glossed case morphology make it feasible. Sheifer & Bolshakova's finding that the ternary strategy spreads to more TAM contexts in Dolgan gives a ready hypothesis.

### Gaps
- I did not systematically map the Sakha literature on evidentiality, converbs, morpheme order or passives. The Sakha column above is incomplete.

### INEL Evenki as a contact source (secondary question)
- INEL Evenki Corpus 2.0 (31 Dec 2024) is by Däbritz, Gusev and Stoynova, edited by Wagner-Nagy and Arkhipov. DOI 10.25592/uhhfdm.16605. — [FDR](https://www.fdr.uni-hamburg.de/record/16605)

  - Dialects: Northern (Taimyr, Khantayskoe Ozero, Ilimpi, Yerbogachyon) with 34,931 tokens; Southern sh-dialects with 55,674 tokens; Southern s-dialects with 2,659 tokens.

### Inferences
- The most defensible novelty is methodological and comparative. It means using the matched INEL tier design across Dolgan and Evenki for quantitative contact-morphology questions (Gaps A and C). Single-construction descriptive papers are now crowded by Däbritz and Siegl.
- Genre and time confound: Dolgan folklore (TDNT, older) vs Stapert's 2008–10 narratives is a confound for any apparent-time claim.
