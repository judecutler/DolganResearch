# Where our studies sit: Dolgan between Sakha, Evenki and Russian

Written by web Claude, 2026-11-06, for the introduction and discussion of the papers. Every source is in `docs/LITERATURE_LEDGER.md` with how it was read. Most were read through automatic text extraction, so check each quote against the original before citing it.

## The setting
Dolgan is a Turkic language very close to Sakha (Yakut). That Evenki influenced Dolgan grammar is a standard claim (Skribnik and Aikhenvald 2024: 1016; Sheifer and Bolshakova 2023). The history of the Dolgan people, including Evenki groups, is the subject of Stapert (2013), which the paper should summarise from the source itself. Today Russian is dominant, and Dolgan is spoken fluently mainly by older people outside a few villages (Pakendorf and Stapert 2020). So each Dolgan feature can be asked three things: is it Turkic (shared with Sakha), Tungusic (shared with Evenki), or Russian-driven? And is it changing?

The INEL corpora let us answer with counts, for Dolgan and Evenki, on the same annotation scheme. Sakha has no comparable corpus here, so Sakha enters from the literature only.

## How the results could fit together
- **Study C (handoffs 008 and 010).** Both are clause-driven. Dolgan near-categorically (partitive in 12.0% of irrealis and 0.3% of realis objects), Evenki gradiently (indefinite accusative 22.3% versus 8.3%), as Kazama (2012, via Siegl 2021) said for Evenki. This contradicts the "Evenki is object-driven" half of Sheifer and Bolshakova (2023) and fits an Evenki-contact account of the Dolgan pattern. The Dolgan restriction is sharper than its supposed model: on the odds scale the clause effect is about 13 times stronger in Dolgan (odds ratio 40.3 against 3.2; ratio 12.7, 95% interval 3.2 to 81.0; 12.5 with the extended irrealis set, 9.1 without pronoun objects). That is the interesting part: a preference that became close to a rule.
- **If B2 or B3 find change with birth year that survives the genre and source controls,** that is change in progress, and the direction (toward Russian-like overt accusative, or toward VO) says whether Russian is the driver. If nothing changes, Dolgan grammar has been stable through a century of shift pressure, which is also worth reporting with the power analysis behind it.
- **Study A, from the glosses alone (handoff 009, exploratory).** PST2 is 77.9% of finite past verbs in folklore, 58.4% in personal narrative and 43.1% in conversation; with first-person subjects it is 47.0% overall and 23.3% in folklore, against 68.3% and 83.4% for third person. Predicative agreement (the "unwitnessed" form for Pakendorf and Stapert 2020) is 82.5% of PST2 in folklore, 58.6% in narrative and 46.5% in conversation. This is the profile of Greed's (2018) last stage: *-BIt* as the non-witnessed narrative tense of folklore, avoided where the speaker narrates their own experience. The folklore-versus-narrative difference is +19.5 points (p = 0.002, speaker-clustered) but +10.2 (p = 0.18) after adjusting for source, because most folklore comes from one source. Your coding decides how many of these uses are evidential. Evenki's glossed `EVID` is rare (0.09 per 1,000 words in folklore, 0.52 in narrative).
