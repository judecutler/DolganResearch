**What this changes for Study A**
- The Evenki literature describes indirect evidentiality mainly through the **perfect**, not through a dedicated `-rkə` suffix. The subagent found no source naming `-rkə`, yet the INEL Evenki corpus glosses 29 tokens of `rkə` as `EVID`. Before comparing, check Nedjalkov (1997) and Bulatova and Grenoble (1999) for this form, and include the Evenki perfect (`PRF`, 110 tokens in 002) among the Evenki candidate forms (D-007).
- The contribution is now narrower: **quantitative and corpus-based**. The qualitative comparison and the contact claim exist in Skribnik and Aikhenvald (2024). Frame the paper as testing their description against corpus data: does Dolgan's lack of reportatives hold? How often are `-TAK` and `e:t` used, by genre and period? Do Dolgan and Evenki use their indirect forms in similar contexts at similar rates? Cite Hagel (2022) as the INEL corpus-method precedent.
- Skribnik and Aikhenvald (2024: 1018) answer two open data questions: **`eːt` is the contracted *e-bit***, and **`-TI` (corpus `PST1`) is the neutral past**, which makes `BIT`/`PST2` the indirective past (D-022, now supported by a source, though the corpus label itself is not discussed there).
