# Study A plan: evidentiality in Dolgan (draft)

Status: **draft.** Owners: Jude and Dustan. Web Claude supplies data tooling and a strawman, nothing more. The categories below are proposals for you to replace.

## Question (draft)

How are evidential and indirective forms distributed in Dolgan by genre and speaker generation? Does the pattern resemble Evenki (contact) or Sakha (relatives)? Dolgan and Evenki are unrelated: Dolgan is Turkic, Evenki is Tungusic. Any similarity is therefore contact or coincidence, never inheritance. The paper must say which it argues for and why.

## What is already known (so we do not re-describe it)
From Däbritz (2023, *Journal of Endangered Languages* 13(23)) and the grammar (Däbritz 2022, §6.6):
- The evidential system is "relatively simple compared to surrounding Siberian languages".
- One dedicated inferential: `-DA4K` plus possessive agreement (grammar p. 296).
- The postterminal `-BI4T` also expresses evidentiality.
- The copula's 3SG postterminal *äbit* has become an evidential particle.
- No Evenki or Sakha comparison and no frequency data were reported.

The paper's contribution is therefore quantitative and comparative. A descriptive repeat of §6.6 would add nothing. **Read §6.6 and the JEL paragraph before coding anything** (see roadmap, Phase 2).

## Gap status
OPEN-VERIFIED for a dedicated or quantitative study of Dolgan evidentiality (no falsifier in 7 queries across English, Russian and German). **Not yet mapped:** the Evenki and Sakha evidential literature. Until WP10 maps it, the comparison claim is unverified. Watch Sheifer's unpublished "Epistemic and agent-oriented modality in Dolgan and Yakut", which is adjacent.

## Data
- **Dolgan:** INEL 2.0, all glossed texts. Genre (folklore, narrative, conversation) is a major confound: folklore uses reportative frames. Control genre throughout.
- **Evenki:** INEL 2.0, by dialect and source (no age information).
- **Optional check:** DoReCo Dolgan (time-aligned; annotations CC BY 4.0) as a validation sample.

## Strawman codebook
**Replace or revise this. It is here so that the first pilot round has something to push against.** Terms come from the general typology of evidentiality. Check them against Däbritz and the Evenki and Sakha sources before relying on them.

| Column | Values | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `is_evidential_use` | `yes`, `no`, `unclear` | The form can have non-evidential uses (tense, perfect, narrative past). This column separates them. |
| `info_source` | `inference-from-result`, `inference-from-reasoning`, `reported`, `mirative-surprise`, `other`, `n/a` | Only if `is_evidential_use = yes`. |
| `person` | `1`, `2`, `3`, `none` | Person of the subject. First-person evidentials are often special. |
| `frame` | `quotation-frame`, `narrative-line`, `background`, `dialogue`, `other` | Whether the form sits in a folklore opening, a story line, and so on. |
| `confidence` | `high`, `medium`, `low` | Your confidence in the coding. |
| `note` | free text | Anything odd. |

Genre, speaker, birth year and source are pre-filled. Do not fill them in.

## Process
1. **Pilot:** about 30 to 50 items, both of you code independently. Compare. Discuss every disagreement and revise the codebook. Repeat if needed. This is Gate 2.
2. **Main round:** the sheet in random order, 15 percent overlap hidden among the items.
3. **Adjudicate:** a third file `evid_adjudicated.csv` for items you disagreed on.
4. **Freeze:** `data-v1`.

## Analysis (draft)
Unit: the evidential form in a text. Plan options:
- rate per 1,000 words by text, with speaker as a grouping factor and token count as exposure
- distribution of `info_source` by genre
- Dolgan against Evenki by genre and dialect, descriptive only (Evenki has no age data)
- cohort effects in Dolgan, as exploration, using the same structure as Study B (`case_change/ANALYSIS_PLAN.md`)

The denominator problem is real: "how often could an evidential have been used?" has no clean answer. Use rates per word and describe the limitation. Do not invent a denominator.
